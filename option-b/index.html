Identify your pest

What's bugging you?

Fire ants, scorpions, termites and mosquitoes are all part of life in Denton County. Here is how to tell the usual visitors apart.

  • Fire ants

    Small reddish-brown ants that build loose dirt mounds in lawns, often after rain. Disturb a mound and many workers come out quickly to sting.

    Denton County is on the Texas Department of Agriculture's imported fire ant quarantine list.

    Get fire ants checked →

  • Scorpions

    The striped bark scorpion is the one you'll find around Denton: about 2½ inches long, tan, with two dark stripes down its back. It hunts at night and hides under rocks, boards and debris by day.

    They're hard to control with insecticides alone, so sealing gaps and clearing debris come first.

    Get scorpions checked →

  • Termites

    Termites live in the soil and travel into homes through thin mud tubes built up foundation walls. Most people first notice a spring swarm of small winged insects, or piles of discarded wings near windows and doors.

    Around Dallas, termite swarms commonly appear in March and April.

    Get termites checked →

  • Mosquitoes

    Southern house mosquitoes breed in standing water like ditches, drains and birdbaths and bite from dusk through the night. Asian tiger mosquitoes breed in flowerpots and clogged gutters and also bite during the day.

    Denton County Public Health tracks West Nile virus in local mosquitoes every season.

    Get mosquitoes checked →

  • Cockroaches

    German cockroaches are small and light brown, with two dark stripes behind the head, and live indoors near kitchens and bathrooms. American cockroaches are much larger and reddish-brown, and wander inside looking for water.

    Get cockroaches checked →

  • Rodents

    Roof rats are climbers that nest high in trees, vines and attics, so scratching overhead at night is often the first clue. House mice are much smaller and can slip through a gap the size of a dime.

    Get rodents checked →

  • Bed bugs

    Flat, wingless, reddish-brown insects about the size of an apple seed. They hide in mattress seams, headboards and nearby furniture during the day, and most people pick them up while traveling.

    Get bed bugs checked →

  • Brown recluse

    A shy, brownish spider with a body ¼ to ½ inch long that hides in closets, boxes, garages and woodpiles. Bites are usually accidental, when one is pressed against skin in stored clothing or bedding.

    Get spiders checked →

Termites and real estate

WDI inspections for buyers, sellers and agents

  • Home buyers
  • Home sellers
  • Real estate agents

A termite inspection for a home sale is reported on the Official Texas Wood Destroying Insect Report (form SPCS/T-5). Reports in a Texas real estate transaction must be on the TDA form.

  • Done by an applicator or technician licensed in the Termite and Wood Destroying Insect Control category.
  • Covers visible, accessible areas. Hidden areas may not be examined.
  • A lender may ask for its own form too. That form is in addition to the Texas form, not instead of it.
  • It is not a warranty and not a structural damage report.
  • Afterward, a sticker with the company name, license number and date goes by the water heater, the breaker box or under the kitchen sink.
  • Before any termite treatment you get a written estimate with the warranty details.
