01Identify your pest

Know what you're looking at.

Fire ants, scorpions, termites and mosquitoes are all part of life in Denton County. Here is how to tell the common ones apart.

  • Fire ants

    Small reddish-brown ants that build loose dirt mounds in lawns, often after rain. Disturb a mound and many workers come out quickly to sting.

    Denton County is on the Texas Department of Agriculture's imported fire ant quarantine list.

  • Scorpions

    The striped bark scorpion is the one you'll find around Denton: about 2½ inches long, tan, with two dark stripes down its back. It hunts at night and hides under rocks, boards and debris by day.

    The only one of Texas's 18 scorpion species found throughout the state.

  • Termites

    They live in the soil and travel into homes through thin mud tubes built up foundation walls. Most homeowners first notice a spring swarm of small winged insects, or piles of wings near windows and doors.

    Around Dallas, termite swarms commonly appear in March and April.

  • Mosquitoes

    Southern house mosquitoes breed in standing water such as ditches, drains and birdbaths and bite from dusk through the night. Asian tiger mosquitoes breed in small containers like flowerpots and clogged gutters and also bite during the day.

    Denton County Public Health tracks West Nile virus in local mosquitoes every season.

  • Cockroaches

    German cockroaches are small and light brown, with two dark stripes behind the head, and live indoors near kitchens and bathrooms. American cockroaches are much larger, live mostly outdoors and in drains, and wander inside looking for water.

  • Rodents

    Roof rats are climbers that nest up high in trees, vines and attics, so scratching overhead at night is often the first clue. House mice are much smaller and can slip through a gap the size of a dime.

    Rats can get in through a hole the size of a quarter.

  • Bed bugs

    Flat, wingless, reddish-brown insects about the size of an apple seed. They hide in mattress seams, headboards and nearby furniture during the day, and most people pick them up while traveling.

  • Brown recluse

    A shy, brownish spider with a body ¼ to ½ inch long that hides in closets, boxes, garages and woodpiles. It is most active at night, and bites are usually accidental, when one is pressed against skin.

Request a WDI inspection

What you get

  • The Official Texas Wood Destroying Insect Report (form SPCS/T-5).
  • An inspection by an applicator or technician licensed in the Termite and Wood Destroying Insect Control category.
  • A sticker with the company name, license number and date, placed by the water heater, breaker box or under the kitchen sink.
  • If your lender asks for its own form, that form is in addition to the Texas form, not instead of it.

What it is not

  • It covers visible, accessible areas. Hidden areas may not be examined.
  • It is not a warranty and not a structural damage report.
